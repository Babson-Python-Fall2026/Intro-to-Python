Finance Primer for PEMS

The few finance ideas you need to build PEMS

OIM3600 · Computational Thinking with AI and Python · Student reference

This is not an investments course. PEMS models a small set of financial events; this primer covers only those.

1. Clients, cash, and shares

A portfolio manager (PM) manages several clients. Each client's portfolio is cash plus holdings: shares of stocks, each identified by a ticker such as AAPL.

PEMS treats cash like a security: its ticker is "$$$$" and its price is always 1.0. So $1,000 of cash is 1,000 "shares" of $$$$.

2. Transactions

A transaction is an event that changes a client's portfolio. PEMS records every transaction and calculates cash and holdings from that record.

TransactionEffectWhen
ContributionCash goes in.Part 1 (cash only)
WithdrawalCash comes out.
BuyCash goes down; shares of a stock go up.Part 2
SellShares of a stock go down; cash goes up.

All amounts are recorded as positive numbers. trans_type says which direction the money moves.

Example. Contribute $1,000, then withdraw $250. Cash is $750. The two transactions are what's stored; $750 is calculated from them.

3. Prices and value

Each trading day (a day the market is open), every stock has a closing price: its price at the end of that day. market_data holds the closing price of every stock for 100 trading days.

Market value of a holding is shares × price. A client's total value is the market value of all holdings plus cash. Value changes when prices change, even if the client does nothing.

PEMS prices are unadjusted. Each price is the price the stock actually traded at on that day. They have not been adjusted for later splits or dividends. That's why PEMS has to apply splits and dividends itself (next section): the prices won't do it for you.

4. Dividends and splits

Companies, not clients, cause these events. They change a client's portfolio without the client doing anything. In market_data, each stock on each day has a div amount and a split ratio.

EventWhat happensExample
Cash dividendThe company pays cash for each share held. The client's cash goes up by shares × dividend per share.100 shares, $0.50 dividend: +$50 cash.
Stock splitEach share becomes several shares (or several become one, in a reverse split). The share count changes; the price changes the opposite way.100 shares at $50, 2-for-1 split: 200 shares at about $25. Still worth about $5,000.

Because prices are unadjusted, a split shows up as a sudden drop in price on the split day. If PEMS didn't also double the shares, the holding would appear to lose half its value. A split changes the number of shares, not the client's wealth.

5. Account growth is not profit

Contributions and withdrawals change how much is in an account, but they aren't investment gains or losses.

Example. A portfolio starts at $10,000. The client contributes $5,000, and the portfolio ends at $16,000. The account grew by $6,000, but the investment gain is only $1,000: the other $5,000 was the client's own money.

6. Check yourself

  4. Why does PEMS need to apply splits itself, instead of relying on the prices?
