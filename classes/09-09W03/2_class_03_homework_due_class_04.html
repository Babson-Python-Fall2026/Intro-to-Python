Class 3 Homework

Practice using AI as a tool for investigation rather than simply collecting answers.

Due at the start of Class 4
The point of this assignment This is not an exercise in getting three answers from Claude. The goal is to practice using AI as a tool for investigation: asking a question, examining the answer, following up, challenging it, and deciding what you now understand.

1. Review the Two Class 2 AI Notebooks

Look back through the two Jupyter notebooks from Class 2.

You do not need to complete every exercise or fill in every response box. Instead, make sure you understand the habits the notebooks are demonstrating.

  • Give the AI enough context.
  • Be clear about what you are trying to understand.
  • Do not automatically accept the first answer.
  • Ask follow-up questions.
  • Ask why.
  • Ask for examples, alternatives, or a simpler explanation.
  • Look for edge cases and assumptions.
  • Verify answers when appropriate.
What you should take away
The notebooks are examples of how to work with AI. They are not material to memorize.

2. Investigate Three of Your Own Questions

You already created two question lists:

Choose 1 AI question Select one question from the three questions you developed after reading the Working with AI workbook.
Choose 2 Python questions Select two questions from the three questions you developed while watching the Python course.

Use Claude in the OIM3600 project to investigate each question.

For each question

  1. Start with your original question.
  2. Read Claude's response carefully.
  3. Ask at least two meaningful follow-up questions.
  4. Continue until you believe you understand the issue better than when you started.

Your follow-up questions should come from the conversation. They might ask:

  • Why does this work?
  • Can you explain that more simply?
  • Can you show me an example?
  • What is an edge case?
  • What could make this answer wrong?
  • Is there another way to think about this?
  • How could I test this in Python?
Avoid mechanical follow-ups
Do not ask questions simply to reach the required number. A useful follow-up should move the investigation forward.

3. Review the OIM3600 Claude Project Instructions

Read through the project instructions that control how Claude behaves in this course.

Do not treat them as fixed or perfect. They were designed for a purpose, and designs can be improved.

Submit a short response answering these three questions:

  1. What is one instruction you think is especially useful? Why?
  2. What is one instruction that seems unclear, unnecessary, too restrictive, or could otherwise be improved?
  3. Suggest one specific change, addition, or deletion that you think would make Claude a better learning partner for this course.
Important
You are not being graded on whether I agree with your suggestion. I am interested in whether you can explain your reasoning.

What to Submit

For each of your three investigations, submit:

  1. Your original question
  2. Your Claude conversation
  3. A short conclusion in your own words:
    • What do you understand now?
    • What was the most useful thing you discovered?
    • Did anything change or correct what you originally thought?

Also submit your short Project Instructions Reflection from Part 3.

The thinking is the deliverable. Claude can generate answers. Your job is to investigate, question, test, and understand them.
